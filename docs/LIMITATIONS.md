# Limitations

## Observer dependence

The protocol measures an encounter, not a substance contained in the text. Results depend on lens, language, context, timing and annotation procedure.

## Resonance is not quality

A false, manipulative or emotionally provocative text may produce high reflection time. E must not be treated as evidence of truth or value.

## Node boundaries are contestable

Annotators may split or merge nodes differently. Node lists and agreement are therefore more informative than N alone.

## Simple dispersion count

Counting interpretation clusters is a pilot simplification. D depends on sample size and clustering rules. Future versions should report normalized entropy and agreement.

## Estimated historical timings

Many historical Lamed Group examples estimated reflection time and simulated reader profiles. These are valuable records of method development, but they are not prospective measurements.

## Evolving scale

Published historical examples used different thresholds and later added coefficients. They must be re-annotated under a frozen protocol before comparative claims are made.

## Circular longevity

Longevity cannot be both evidence used to calibrate density and an independent coefficient multiplied into that density. T is therefore excluded from the v0.1 core.

## Model instability

AI outputs vary across model releases, system instructions, context windows and sampling. Model observations require dates and available configuration metadata.

## Translation

Translation may change node structure, dispersion and resonance. A translation is a new construct and requires a new annotation.

## No benchmark claim

The pilot corpus is not a leaderboard and does not establish scientific validity. It is an auditable starting point for testing the protocol.
