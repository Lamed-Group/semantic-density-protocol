# Ashby, requisite variety and the semantic lens

## Why Ashby matters here

W. Ross Ashby's law of requisite variety states a limit on regulation: a regulator cannot suppress or control more variety than it can carry as a channel. Ashby also noted that observers may estimate variety differently because they can make different distinctions.

Applied to semantic analysis:

- the construct presents possible distinctions and relations;
- the lens acts as an observing and decoding channel;
- context changes the distinctions available to that lens;
- structure that the lens cannot distinguish is experienced as ambiguity, noise, banality or absence.

The mapping is a methodological analogy and constraint. It is not a claim that semantic density is identical to Shannon entropy.

## Constraint

Let:

- V_C denote the variety required to unfold a construct;
- V_L denote the variety of distinctions available to a lens.

Then:

    V_L < V_C

implies that the observation is resolution-limited. The missing structure cannot be recovered merely by assigning a larger score.

The protocol therefore records:

- nodes found and nodes missed;
- observer or model profile;
- language and cultural code;
- domain familiarity;
- amount of context;
- non-answer or collapse;
- variation across lenses.

## Message and noise

In Ashby's cybernetic framing, noise is not intrinsically different from other variety; the distinction depends on the recipient and the goal. This is directly relevant to a dense construct that one observer reads as a signal and another reads as noise.

The protocol does not decide that one observer is globally superior. It records which distinctions became available under which conditions.

## Lens profile

A lens profile SHOULD include:

    lens_id
    lens_type
    language
    domain_familiarity
    construct_familiarity
    cultural_context
    context_level
    analysis_mode
    model_label_and_date_if_ai
    instructions_summary_if_ai

No universal scalar "lens power" is defined in v0.1.

## Multi-lens result

The preferred result is a profile rather than one score:

- central observed density;
- spread across lenses;
- stable nodes shared by lenses;
- lens-specific nodes;
- context-sensitive nodes;
- non-recognition rate.

High cross-lens spread is itself evidence. It may indicate a construct whose semantic topology depends strongly on prior distinctions.
